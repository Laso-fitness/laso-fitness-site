DoD / USSOCOM Human-Performance Research

I-ART

Intentional Adrenaline Response Training — a proposed 12-week human-performance research protocol investigating whether voluntary autonomic regulation can be progressively trained, measured, and integrated with physical training in high-stress operational environments.

Principal Investigator: Reginald J. Gardiner II
Laso 360 LLC
USSOCOM Biomedical & Human Performance R&D
Research concept

Train the response, not just the recovery.

I-ART combines controlled breathing and autonomic-regulation sequences with wearable HRV monitoring, progressive Laso 360 center-of-gravity loading, stress-inoculation concepts, and digital protocol delivery. The goal is to create a measurable, repeatable training framework for voluntary regulation under high-stress conditions.

Physiology

Wearable HRV measures including RMSSD, SDNN and LF/HF are incorporated into baseline and longitudinal assessment.

Performance

Operationally relevant performance measures can be paired with physiological data to evaluate whether regulation training transfers to performance.

Movement

Laso 360 center-of-gravity loading is designed to add resistance while preserving natural movement and athletic mechanics.

Digital delivery

App-E-Tight, Blue Marvel and Coach Command Center provide a foundation for structured protocol delivery, session logging and performance oversight.

Development pathway

Phase I → Phase II

  • Phase I: feasibility and safety assessment with a proposed 12-week protocol and physiological/performance measurements.
  • Measurement: HRV at defined protocol checkpoints, combined with operational-performance benchmarks.
  • Phase II: a pathway to a larger randomized study design, power analysis and formal research execution if supported by Phase I results and funding.
  • Translation: field-deployable, self-administered training designed around wearable measurement and digital delivery.
Why this work: More than two decades of applied human-performance practice, a U.S. patent in human loading technology, biomechanics and movement analysis, wearable-data integration, AI-assisted fitness technology, and real-world coaching experience inform the proposed system.
